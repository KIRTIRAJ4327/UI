# Platform owner review: Unified Product Delivery Platform (prototype at :4174)

## 1. Verdict
This is a convincing delivery workflow with a solid governance layer: named squads with backups, SoD toggles, approval targets with escalation, change freezes, a hash-chained audit log, and an agent budget. It is not yet a complete platform. Everything an enterprise needs to adopt it and run it is either hard-coded or missing: the 3 applications are fixed, the 8 stages and 7 gates cannot be configured, environments exist only as the word "Production", the model is a read-only "GPT-5.4", and there is no API, webhooks, reporting or billing. The biggest risk to the deal is the trust story. The evidence record contradicts the audit log (see below), and rollback is promised in the UI but cannot be done anywhere. A buyer's risk or audit team will find both in the first demo, and that undermines the "every decision is evidenced" pitch that justifies buying.

Evidence for the trust risk (seen in the running prototype):
- #v3-evidence says the ADR was "Saved Wed 12 Nov, 14:33". Today is Tue 11 Nov, and the audit log says ADR-0173 was saved 11 Nov 09:42 UTC (Evidence.jsx:33).
- The Evidence tiles still use CountUp (Evidence.jsx:18), even though round 4 says count-up was removed from data tiles. A capture taken mid-animation showed "5 of 6 quality checks / 12.6 min / $0.19", while the run page shows "6 of 6 / 13 min 48 s".
- WI-4751 has four release dates: "Released 6 Nov" (Runs), "09 Nov 16:05" (Audit), "Mon 16:05" (store) and "Tue 16:05" (Home activity). Notification n5 says "Fee schedule update released · WI-4751", but fee schedule is WI-4772 and it has not been released.
- #v3-release says "You can roll back for 24 hours after release from the run page". #v3-run-wi-4751 (released) has no rollback control, no production health check and no incident link.

## 3. Value and metrics for the leadership dashboard
- Lead time from request to production (median and p85) per app, against the pre-platform baseline captured at onboarding. This is the headline "cycle time saved".
- Time split: agent work vs waiting for people, per stage. The prototype already says "Agent work takes about 2 hours; most of 3 days is approvals". Make that the chart, and add hours per role (Admin "Where work waits" is the seed).
- Approval SLA attainment per role and person, escalations, and reassignments due to leave.
- Throughput: requests started, released and abandoned per week. WIP and the blocked count.
- Quality: first-pass gate approval rate (approved vs "request changes"), agent self-corrections, validation failure rate, change failure rate, rollbacks, time to restore. These 4 DORA metrics are the language a CIO buys in.
- Cost: agent spend per released change and per app, against the estimated engineering hours avoided (value = hours avoided x blended rate - agent spend - licence).
- Governance health: % of runs with full evidence chain, SoD violations prevented, freeze exceptions granted, rule coverage (for example RA-MAX "checked in 2 of 6 places" rolled up across all rules).
- Adoption: active requesters per business unit, % of changes that came in through the platform vs bypassed it.

## 4. Top 5 recommendations (ranked)
1. Fix the trust defects before any client demo. Make the Evidence "Saved" time and figures match the audit log and remove CountUp there. Make the WI-4751 release date consistent everywhere and fix the n5 notification. Either build "Roll back" on the released run page or remove the promise. These are S effort and protect the core value claim.
2. Add an Applications onboarding wizard and a workspace switcher (repos, pipeline, environments, rules source, default squad and policies). This is the difference between a demo of 3 apps and a platform the customer can roll out themselves.
3. Add a Workflows screen with risk-tiered templates (stages and gates on or off, required checks, 2-person release, low-risk fast path). Tie it to Policies so SoD and freezes apply per template. This answers "is this overkill for a label change?" and "is it strict enough for pricing?".
4. Add an "AI and data" admin page plus a global agent kill switch: model per stage, data residency, redaction, no-training statement, agent write scopes, and pause-all. Pair it with a security and trust one-pager. This clears the CISO gate.
5. Add an Insights dashboard for leadership (section 3), with a baseline captured at onboarding, plus Plan and usage. This gives the economic buyer an ROI story ("lead time 9 days to 3.2 days; $X agent spend per change") and makes pricing tiers concrete: Team (1 app, fixed workflow), Business (multi-app, custom workflows, API), Enterprise (compliance packs, custom roles, residency, SSO/SCIM).
