Part 1

Three ways to run AI-assisted delivery

A. This platform (prototype): a business request goes through eight gated stages with named approvers and one evidence trail; the Developer Agent runs standalone in VS Code. B. Spec-driven in the repo: GitHub Spec Kit and AWS Kiro, where developers write and approve markdown specifications next to the code. C. IDE only: everyone works from VS Code with an AI coding agent, plus Azure DevOps or GitHub for pull requests and pipelines.

Score by role, out of 12

Four criteria, 0 to 3 each: works in their natural tool, understands what the AI did, control (approve, stop, send back), evidence kept automatically. Our assessment; the platform is scored as a prototype, the other two as shipping products. No users were observed or timed.

Average by criterion, 0 to 3

The platform leads on evidence and on explaining the AI's work. IDE-only working leads on staying in the person's natural tool.

One small change, walked through all three

The change: block values above 310 on one form. Counts are for a regulated organisation with tester and release roles. In a small team, approaches B and C need 2 tools, 1 wait and 1 recorded approval. Elapsed time was not measured.

What the experience is like

This platform

  • Non-developers work in a browser, in plain language, and never open a repository.
  • Status, who holds the work and what happens next are always visible.
  • Every approval is explained by "How the agent did this" and recorded.
  • Cost: more gates and more waiting; developers leave their editor to pick up work.

Spec-driven in the repo

  • Natural for developers: specifications are files next to the code.
  • Product owners and auditors must read markdown in a repository or rely on a developer.
  • Published reviews describe long specifications that are heavier to review than the code.
  • Approval is a pull-request review; nothing is approved before code exists.

IDE only

  • Fastest loop for a developer, with no new process to learn.
  • Other roles see only tickets and pull requests, not what the AI did or why.
  • Evidence is spread across the editor, the repository and the pipeline.
  • Works well for small teams and developer-only changes.

Regulation that shapes delivery

  • 13 July 2026: OSFI bulletin on generative and agentic AI, covering technology, cyber security and operational resilience, including software development.
  • 1 May 2027: OSFI Guideline E-23 on model risk takes effect and covers AI models.
  • Existing guidelines B-13 (technology and cyber risk) and B-10 (third-party risk) already apply to change control and to vendors.
  • There is no federal AI statute in force, so OSFI guidance is what binds.

Region by region

Dot: our estimate of where most bank engineering work sits. Line: plausible range. Confidence is medium for the US, UK, Australia, Canada and the EU, and low elsewhere.

  • United States (about 2.0): the leading banks show the most public evidence of coding agents in daily engineering work. Model-risk guidance leaves generative and agentic AI out of scope.
  • United Kingdom and Australia (about 1.75): wide use of assistants and some agent programmes; UK firms keep agents on a short leash, with 2% allowing broad autonomy.
  • European Union (about 1.5): the AI Act and DORA put change control and evidence first.
  • Asia-Pacific, Middle East, Latin America (1.0 to 1.5): evidence is thinner and confidence is low.

What it means for a Canadian bank

  • The platform targets a level no bank has publicly reached. There is no peer to copy, so plan a staged pilot with its own baseline.
  • It lines up with what OSFI asks for. Map each gate and evidence record to the expectation it meets, and put the platform's own agents in the model inventory before May 2027.
  • Eight gates will collide with the review bottleneck unless gates are risk-tiered from day one.
  • The value is at the two ends of the lifecycle, the business request and the release evidence. Banks already have coding tools.
  • Keeping the developer agent in VS Code fits the tools Canadian banks have disclosed, but the hand-off must be tracked.
  • Measure delivery outcomes (lead time, change failure rate, review wait, send-back rate) and expect a dip in the first months.
  • Treat the vendor and model providers as critical third parties and plan where prompts and code are processed.

Method and limits

  • Part 1 is an assessment by an analyst reading the prototype and public documentation of Spec Kit, Kiro and IDE agents. No users were observed.
  • Part 2 rests on about 95 web searches on 12 November 2026. No primary page could be opened, so every figure comes from a search excerpt or secondary coverage.
  • Bank figures are self-reported and not comparable with each other. Vendor telemetry studies come from companies that sell a remedy for the problem they measure.
  • Not found: any survey figure for AI use in requirements or design; Evident point scores; the exact wording of OSFI's bulletin on AI-generated code.
